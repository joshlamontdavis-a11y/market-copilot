# Market brief: A wireless smart sprinkler controller that replaces a home's irrigation timer and adjusts watering to local weather, controlled from a phone app

Research date: 2026-10-27. Prices and competitors change, so check the links before relying on them.

Target customer: Homeowners with in-ground sprinkler systems. Geography: United States. Depth: quick.

This brief prepares you for customer interviews. It does not show that anyone will buy.

Verified means the quoted text is on the cited page. Read each quote to judge whether it supports the claim.

## 1. Summary

Checked by check.py: 17 verified, 12 assumptions, 0 unverified.

### What we found

- **[Verified]** EPA estimates more than 28 million US homes have in-ground sprinkler systems that typically schedule watering with a clock-based controller. (S1)
- **[Verified]** EPA says replacing a clock-based controller with a WaterSense labeled weather-based controller can save an average home nearly 7,600 gallons of water a year. (S2)
- **[Verified]** A product reviewer describes Rachio and Orbit B-hyve as being in a two-way race for smart sprinkler market share. (S14)
- **[Verified]** Established smart controllers list at or below $200: Rachio 3 8-zone normally $199 at Home Depot, Rain Bird ST8 $160, Orbit B-hyve 6-zone $90-$115. (S6, S12, S8)
- **[Verified]** Some water utilities offer rebates for smart controllers; one buyer reported paying $15 for a Rachio after a water rebate. (S3, S7)
- **[Verified]** SkyDrop tried to move features advertised as free behind a monthly subscription. (S13)
- **[Verified]** A University of Minnesota Extension study found 75 percent of homes with irrigation systems had at least one broken sprinkler. (S16)
- **[Assumption]** At $200 one-time, the product would be priced at the top of an established category, so it needs a reason to win beyond weather-based scheduling, which incumbents already offer.
- **[Assumption]** Broken heads and leaks could be a stronger pain point to build around than scheduling, since a smart schedule does not fix a broken sprinkler.

### What we still don't know

- What share of the 28 million homes already own a smart controller.
- How often homeowners replace their irrigation controller (replacement cycle).
- How many homeowners program their own controller versus relying on a landscaper or irrigation contractor.
- Current US unit sales of smart controllers and each brand's share.
- How many water utilities offer rebates today and how large they are.
- Rachio's direct price on rachio.com (the product page shows no price to scripts).
- How current the competitor prices are: the Rachio deal post is from April 2026, the GearBrain roundup from April 2025, and the Rain Bird price from a 2020 review.

## 2. Riskiest assumptions

1. **Homeowners will pay $200 for a new brand when Rachio and B-hyve already do weather-based watering at the same price or less.**
   - Why it's risky: The sourced prices show incumbents at $90-$199, and rebates can bring them near zero.
   - How to test it: Ask 5 homeowners who bought a smart controller what they paid, what else they considered, and why they chose it.
2. **Homeowners with clock-based timers feel enough pain to replace a working timer.**
   - Why it's risky: EPA says most such homes still use clock-based controllers, which may mean the problem is not urgent to them.
   - How to test it: Ask 5 homeowners with old timers when they last changed the schedule and what, if anything, went wrong.
3. **The homeowner, not a landscaper or irrigation contractor, chooses and installs the controller.**
   - Why it's risky: If contractors decide, a consumer app and retail channel would miss the buyer.
   - How to test it: Ask homeowners who set up or replaced their current controller, and talk to 2 irrigation contractors about what they install.
4. **A one-time price with no subscription can fund ongoing weather data and app costs.**
   - Why it's risky: SkyDrop tried to add a subscription after launch; cloud costs continue after the sale.
   - How to test it: Model per-user cloud and weather API cost for 10 years and compare it to gross margin at $200.

## 3. Interview script

Thanks for talking with me. I'm learning how homeowners look after their lawn watering. There are no right answers, and I'm not selling anything.

1. Tell me about the last time you changed your sprinkler schedule. What made you do it?
   - Listen for: How often they touch the timer, and whether weather or water bills trigger changes.
2. Who set up your current sprinkler controller, and when?
   - Listen for: Whether the homeowner, a builder, or a contractor chooses and installs controllers.
3. Tell me about the last time something went wrong with your sprinklers.
   - Listen for: Broken heads, leaks, overwatering in rain, dead patches, and what it cost them.
4. What did you spend on watering your lawn last summer, including water bills and repairs?
   - Listen for: Whether water cost is a felt pain and how large it is.
5. Have you ever looked into a smart or Wi-Fi sprinkler controller? What happened?
   - Listen for: Brands they know, why they bought or did not buy, price objections, rebate awareness.
6. If you own a smart controller, how did you choose it, and what do you wish it did?
   - Listen for: Unmet needs incumbents leave open, and what made them pick a brand.
7. How do you handle watering restrictions or rebates from your water utility today?
   - Listen for: Whether utilities shape what they buy and when.
